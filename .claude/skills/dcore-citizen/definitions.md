# Definitions

The words the report is built from. Change a definition or a label here; the
steps in `SKILL.md` stay the same. Labels in **bold** are what the page shows,
so they must stay plain words.

## River (thread)

A **thread** is one line of related talk on one question: a question asked,
the replies, the follow-ups. A conversation has several threads. They run side
by side, pause, and come back later; a return to a thread is the same thread,
not a new one.

- Name each thread by the question it is about, in words both sides would
  accept ("What must the Commission answer?").
- Talk that belongs to no thread is either a distraction (it pulled a thread
  off course) or idle (intro, ads, tech trouble, goodbyes).
- 3 to 6 threads. Merge small ones into the thread they serve.

## Where a thread ended

Every thread ends in exactly one state, judged at the end of the conversation.

| State | Page label | Means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `converged` | **Agreed** | Both sides accepted a point, a fact, a test or a next step. |
| `clarified` | **Clearer** | Not agreed, but the disagreement got sharper: named, split into parts, or narrowed to one thing. |
| `bounded` | **Agreed to differ** | Both sides said plainly where they differ and why (often a value), and left it there. |
| `stuck` | **Stuck** | Talked about, but ended where it started. |
| `dropped` | **Dropped** | Abandoned after a distraction, and never came back. |

Progress has two kinds and both count: **convergence** (agreement) and
**clarity** (a sharper disagreement). A thread that gets neither is the real
failure.

## Progress (good moves)

A good move moves a thread toward a shared next step. Plain labels in bold.

- Convergence: **agreed**, **gave ground** (concession), **agreed in part**,
  **changed position**, **restated the other side fairly** (and they accepted).
- Evidence: **gave a checkable fact**, **proposed a test**, **agreed what would
  change their mind**, **asked for the data**.
- Structure: **named the real question**, **defined a word both use**,
  **narrowed the question**, **split the question in two**, **said where they
  differ** ("we differ only on X").
- Action: **took on a next step**, **proposed a trial**, **offered a
  trade-off**.
- Process: **set it aside with a condition**, **named who should decide**,
  **separated facts from values**.
- Answers: **answered the question**, and the answer closes it (see Answer
  that left a gap). Not being challenged is not enough.

## Bid

A **bid** is something one side puts on the table that asks the other side to
engage with it:

- a **question** ("What do you mean by harm?"), or
- a **point** aimed at the other side that carries the thread forward ("Your
  own data shows costs fell").

A passing remark is not a bid. Only a point that is aimed at the other side
and carries weight for its thread counts.

## Unanswered bid

A bid that got **no reply at all**: nobody answered it, argued with it, or
built on it. It stops progress as surely as a distraction does, only quietly.
The page shows it in orange, apart from distractions (red).

- **Owner:** the voice the bid was aimed at. If it was aimed at nobody in
  particular (put to the whole panel), the owner is the moderator, for not
  following up.
- **Signal, not noise:** count only bids that carry weight (see Bid). Mark
  the ones that could have led to a step as **could have led somewhere**;
  those are the signal the page leads with.
- A reply that talked past the bid is not "unanswered"; it is a dodge
  (**Talked past it**). Unanswered means silence.

## Answer that left a gap

A reply that **engages with the bid but leaves a specific part of it open**.
Not a dodge and not silence: something real was said, and something specific
is still missing. It looks like progress, so it is easy to miss. The page
shows it as a hollow orange dot: answered on paper, still open.

| Gap | Page label |
| --- | --- |
| partial | **Answered part of it** |
| unsupported | **A conclusion, no reason** |
| circular | **Repeated the claim as the reason** |
| promise | **Promised instead of answering** |
| shifted | **Gave intent, not the fact asked for** |
| nonsequitur | **The reason doesn't lead there** |

- **Owner:** the voice that answered. The follow-up that would close the gap
  belongs in the same entry; it is the missed move for the asker or the
  moderator, not a separate dot.
- **Where the line falls:** **Talked past it** never touches the question.
  **Vague non-answer** says nothing anyone could check. **Left a gap** says
  something specific and leaves something specific out.
- A gap judges the shape of the answer, never how convincing it is. "I
  disagree, because X" is complete if X bears on the question, however weak X
  looks.

## Distraction

A **distraction** is anything that pulls a thread away from progress. There
are two kinds.

### Dodge: the reply to a bid did not engage with it

The victim is the person whose bid went unanswered.

| Kind | Page label |
| --- | --- |
| deflected | **Changed the subject** |
| attacked | **Attacked the asker, not the question** |
| ignored | **Talked past it** |
| diluted | **Vague non-answer** |

### Unprompted: a move that pulled the thread off course on its own

The victim is the thread itself.

| Kind | Page label |
| --- | --- |
| slogan | **Slogans and numbers with no source** |
| overclaim | **Claimed more than the evidence shows** |
| motive | **Attacked motives, not the argument** |
| mockery | **Mocked instead of answering** |
| strawman | **Twisted the other side's point** |
| goalposts | **Moved the goalposts** |
| loaded | **Loaded question** |
| repeat | **Same point repeated** |
| heat | **Raised the temperature** |
| host-side | **Host took a side** (moderator only) |
| host-detour | **Host let a detour run** (moderator only) |

Add a label here when a new kind keeps coming up; keep it plain and keep it
describing an action, never a person.

### Not a distraction

- Emotion that brings out a real stake or value ("this affects my family").
  It often reveals the value the thread is really about, so it can be
  progress.
- A change of topic that both sides accept, or that the moderator makes to
  move on after a thread has ended.
- Coming back to an earlier thread.

## Missed opportunity

A **missed opportunity** is progress that was within reach at a moment and did
not happen. "Within reach" means it could be built in one or two moves from
what was already said up to that moment: claims made, facts given, things
conceded. No outside knowledge, no smarter participants.

## Replay

A **replay** is one fair way a thread could have gone: the same voices, the
same views, but each one engages with every bid, backs claims or says they are
unchecked, and gives ground when a point lands. It is one plausible path, not
the right answer, and it shows how close the better outcome was.

## Time

Every second of the run time belongs to one type.

| Type | Page label | Means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `fwd` | **Progress** | A good move happened (agreed, answered, clarified). |
| `talk` | **On topic, but left open** | On a thread, but no one replied, or the answer left a gap. |
| `side` | **Distractions** | A distraction, carrying its label. |
| `idle` | **Intro and breaks** | Intro, ads, tech trouble, goodbyes. |
