# Tone and reading experience

How the report speaks and how it should feel to read. Change these to tune
the voice; the steps in `SKILL.md` stay the same.

## The reader

A busy citizen with little time and little attention, reading on a phone.
They should get the whole picture in about a minute without tapping anything,
and find the proof behind any line in one tap. The aim is a habit: next time
they watch a debate, they ask "was the question answered, did anyone reply,
did anyone agree?" instead of repeating the slogan.

## Principles (never break these)

- **Take no sides.** Grade moves, never positions. Anyone may argue for or
  against anything. Criticism lands on every side where it is earned, the host
  included. Never discourage disagreement, only moves that pull a thread off
  course.
- **Actions, not labels.** Say what was done ("changed the subject"), never
  what someone is ("liar", "propagandist").
- **Roles, not names.** Name voices by their role in this conversation (Host,
  Ruling party, Opposition, Analyst, Former official, Resident 1). People who
  are talked about are named by office where that is enough.
- **Claims are reported, not endorsed.** "Said 13 crore names were removed."
  Numbers quoted by speakers are unchecked unless you checked them.
- **The replay is one fair path, not what happened.** Say so in plain words in the
  footer and the guide; don't label it "simulated" on the page.

## Wording

- **Plain words only.** No debate jargon: "Changed the subject", not "red
  herring"; "Attacked motives, not the argument", not "ad hominem". Use the
  labels in `definitions.md`.
- **Every line stands alone.** People skim. No "it", "they", "this" without
  the noun. Name the institution, the question, the thing.
- **Specific over general.** "The question about the commissioners' majority
  was never answered", not "key questions went unanswered".
- **Don't overgeneralise.** Write what the conversation shows, no more.
- **No motive, no feeling.** Never say someone was frustrated, afraid or
  dishonest. If a speaker named a motive, report it as their claim.
- **Same directness for everyone.** Read each line as its subject would. If it
  would sting without adding information, rewrite it.
- **Language.** Write in the language the user asks for (default English),
  whatever the transcript's language. Translate quotes faithfully.

## Making it effortless

- **Pictures first, words as labels.** The collapsed page is bars, dots,
  lanes and stamps with short captions.
- **One idea per line.** Captions under 15 words. Step texts under 12.
- **Detail on demand.** Every judgement has a ▸ toggle that opens the
  timestamped moments in place. Nothing important lives only behind a toggle;
  the proof does.
- **Colour means one thing everywhere.** Green: progress that really
  happened. Red: missed or distracted. Orange: left unanswered. Hollow
  orange: answered, but left a gap.
  Black: intro and breaks. Never show a missed step in green.
- **No numbers for people.** Percentages for time are fine; never scores or
  grades for a voice. Dots and words instead.
- **Who did it, behind the toggle.** On the collapsed page, a missed step
  shows when it was lost and how ("Lost at 16:29 · Changed the subject"); the
  name of the voice sits in the detail and in the voices section. Change this
  rule here if the report should name voices up front.
- **Every timestamp is a link** to the video at that second, or to the
  transcript line.

## Before publishing, check

- Does each line stand alone?
- Is every judgement traceable to a real moment?
- Is any side spared criticism it earned, or given criticism it didn't?
- Is any wording overgeneralised, jargon, or a label on a person?
- Can the collapsed page be read in about a minute?
